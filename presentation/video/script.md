## 01 · Title

Every big lab says it's building AGI. A machine that thinks the way we do. I don't think they can get there.

## 02 · The wrong shape

Not because the models aren't big enough, or the data isn't good enough. Because the thing they're building is the wrong shape.

## 03 · Senses in

Start with you. You have inputs: eyes, ears, skin. That's how you detect the world.

## 04 · Actions out

You have outputs: a mouth, hands. That's how you act on the world.

## 05 · The hidden middle

And in between there's a part nobody can see from outside. We call it the brain.

## 06 · AI copies the outside

Modern AI copies this picture. Text in, text out, something in the middle. The outside is right; it's the middle they got wrong.

## 07 · The tangle

Here's what the middle actually looks like in you. Senses come in on one side, actions go out the other, and in between is a tangle: a mass of neurons, each connected to many others. Nobody assigned the connections a direction.

## 08 · Signals go everywhere

A signal can go from any neuron to any other. Forward, backward, sideways. Activity circulates, and a thought can feed back into itself and keep going.

## 09 · The pipeline

Now today's models. They are built like a pipeline: the signal enters at layer one, moves to layer two, then three, and leaves at the end. It only ever moves forward.

## 10 · Nothing flows back

Nothing flows back. Nothing circulates. Even a transformer only goes one way, and it ends at the top.

## 11 · A calculator

That's not how a brain is structured. That's how a calculator is structured.

## 12 · Parable: the factory line

Picture today's AI as a factory. Your text rides a conveyor belt, each machine works on it once, in order, and a finished answer drops off the end. A brain is more like a village. Everyone talks to everyone, back and forth, until the mayor announces what the town has decided. Your neurons are the villagers, and whatever you say or do is the announcement. You are the sum of every decision those little guys make.

## 13 · A brain never stops

The second difference follows from the first. Because a brain can loop, it never sits idle waiting for input: whatever you see, hear or read lands in a mind that is already running. From birth to death you are thinking; the only time you stop is when you die.

## 14 · A thought can't be measured

Nobody can measure how a thought happens. We cannot see it from outside. We only see what comes out.

## 15 · How a transformer works

This is a simplified picture. In each layer, every word looks back at the words before it. Then everything moves up a layer, and one word comes out the top. To get the next word, it runs the whole thing again.

## 16 · What it can't do

What it cannot do is go back. Once a signal has passed a layer, nothing returns to rethink it. The only new thing it carries over to the next step is that one word.

## 17 · Text is not a chain of thought

So all it ever carries forward is text: your words, and the words it has written so far. And text is not a chain of thought.

## 18 · What a brain looks like

Let me show you what a brain is supposed to look like. Open that pipe up into a space with no one-way road: signals bounce in every direction, split apart, merge into something bigger, and burst into something new. Nothing ever comes out the far end; it just keeps going.

## 19 · A mind never restarts

A mind does not restart. Signals run through a web of billions of connections, back and forth, all at once, with no beginning and no end. Nothing is written out and fed back in; it simply keeps going.

## 20 · Problem two

That's problem one. Problem two is about what a thought even is.

## 21 · A thought is not text

A thought is not a sentence, and it's not a picture. Right now you can feel several things, picture several things, and reach back to several moments, all at once. Together they are one thing that never holds still, and none of it is words.

## 22 · Parable: the strawberry

Try describing the taste of a strawberry to someone who has never had one. Sour, fresh, sweet, juicy, a summer afternoon, your grandmother's jam: all of it comes to mind at once. You can say sweet, a little sour; the words are true, and they miss almost everything.

## 23 · A thought can't be described

When you try to describe a thought you lose most of it. And the way one thought turns into the next can't be described at all.

## 24 · This is not thinking

People read "let me think step by step" and see a mind at work. There is no mind at work: nothing is kept inside, an input runs through an enormous calculation and comes out as text, and then it is finished. Feed the text back in and another calculation runs; we don't even know what a thought is, but this is not one.

## 25 · Parable: the elephant

Let me tell you a famous old story, because it fits exactly where we stand with thought. Three blind men are brought to an elephant. None of them has ever met one, and none of them can see it.

## 26 · The trunk

The first one took hold of the trunk. "An elephant," he said, "is a kind of snake."

## 27 · The leg

The second one wrapped his arms around a leg. "No," he said, "an elephant is a tree."

## 28 · The tail

The third one caught the tail. "You are both wrong. An elephant is a rope."

## 29 · Nobody has seen the whole

Each of them touched something real, and each of them was sure. None of them knew what an elephant is. That is where we stand with thought: every word we have for it is one hand on one part, and nobody has ever seen the whole.

## 30 · The middle is a filter

Look at what happens between the input and the output. Text enters, passes through fixed layers, leaves as text. Nothing is held; the only state that survives is the words already produced.

## 31 · Breed what you cannot draw

None of this is new. If you want to build a mind, something whose structure and workings you cannot specify in advance, you have to stop trying to design it by hand. Instead, you turn to nature's oldest method: evolution and genetics. Generate many random variants, keep the ones that perform best, and let their offspring inherit those traits, with small mutations. Researchers have evolved programs, virtual creatures, and even the wiring of neural networks this way for decades.

## 32 · A network that never stops

A brain-like network shouldn't run once and stop. In a recurrent network every signal can loop back, so activity keeps circulating after the input is gone. A pattern can stay alive in those loops, and the network can follow things that unfold over time.

## 33 · A network that remembers

It can also remember. Give every neuron a value it keeps from moment to moment, fading at its own pace: some forget at once, others hold on. So what happened a while ago still shapes what happens now.

## 34 · Outro

Not one of these ideas is new. Scientists worked them out decades ago, and we are coming back to them now because the technology has finally caught up. We already know roughly how a mind that really thinks is made, because nature made us that way, and we survived by its rules. What we don't know is the details. So we have to let go of designing every part, and let it grow. That's the next video.

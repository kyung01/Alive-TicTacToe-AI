06 [matter-of-fact] Modern AI copies this picture. Text in, text out, something in the middle. [pointed] The outside is right; it's the middle they got wrong.
07 [fascinated] Here's what the middle actually looks like in you. Senses come in on one side, actions go out the other, and in between is a tangle: a mass of neurons, each connected to many others. Nobody assigned the connections a direction.
08 [energetic] A signal can go from any neuron to any other. Forward, backward, sideways. Activity circulates, and a thought can feed back into itself and keep going.
09 [dry, explanatory] Now today's models. They are built like a pipeline: the signal enters at layer one, moves to layer two, then three, and leaves at the end. It only ever moves forward.
10 [flatly] Nothing flows back. Nothing circulates. Even a transformer only goes one way... and it ends at the top.
11 [wry] That's not how a brain is structured. That's how a calculator is structured.
12 [storytelling] Picture today's AI as a factory. Your text rides a conveyor belt, each machine works on it once, in order, and a finished answer drops off the end. [warmer] A brain is more like a village. Everyone talks to everyone, back and forth, until the mayor announces what the town has decided. [confident] Your neurons are the villagers, and whatever you say or do is the announcement. You are the sum of every decision those little guys make.
13 [matter-of-fact, clear full voice] The second difference follows from the first. Because a brain can loop, it never sits idle waiting for input: whatever you see, hear or read lands in a mind that is already running. From birth to death you are thinking. [firm] The only time you stop is when you die.
14 [matter-of-fact] Nobody can measure how a thought happens. We cannot see it from outside. We only see what comes out.
15 [explanatory] This is a simplified picture. In each layer, every word looks back at the words before it. Then everything moves up a layer, and one word comes out the top. To get the next word, it runs the whole thing again.
16 [serious] What it cannot do is go back. Once a signal has passed a layer, nothing returns to rethink it. The only new thing it carries over to the next step is that one word.
17 [firm] So all it ever carries forward is text: your words, and the words it has written so far. And text is not a chain of thought.
18 [excited, building] Let me show you what a brain is supposed to look like. [awed] Nothing ever comes out the far end; it just keeps going.
19 [calm, awed] A mind does not restart. Signals run through a web of billions of connections, back and forth, all at once, with no beginning and no end. Nothing is written out and fed back in; it simply keeps going.

30 [analytical] Look at what happens between the input and the output. Text enters, passes through fixed layers, leaves as text. [flatly] Nothing is held; the only state that survives is the words already produced.
31 [hopeful] None of this is new. If you want to build a mind, something whose structure and workings you cannot specify in advance, you have to stop trying to design it by hand. 32 [engaged] A brain-like network shouldn't run once and stop. In a recurrent network every signal can loop back, so activity keeps circulating after the input is gone. A pattern can stay alive in those loops, and the network can follow things that unfold over time.
